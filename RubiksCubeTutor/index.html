    About Cube Clubhouse

    What this is

    Cube Clubhouse is designed to help anyone, of any age, learn to solve a Cube on their own, using the beginner "layer by layer" method that most people learn first. It is for general audiences: adults learning for the first time, families, classrooms and clubs all use the same lessons. It is a teaching app, not a game: no lesson is ever timed, there is nothing to buy and nobody to talk to. The one clock that keeps score is the optional Timer, and the only person it compares you with is yourself.

    What is in it

    • Learn — ten lessons, in order, from "what the pieces are" to the last twist. Each one has a cube you can turn, the trick written in plain words you can say out loud, and a practice cube that checks your work and awards up to three stars. Reset Cube puts the practice cube back to how the puzzle started.
    • Play — a free-play cube from 2×2 up to 6×6, with mix-up, undo and a timer. Help Me Solve It walks a mixed cube on the screen back to solved, one card at a time, at a speed you pick, with pause and stop.
    • Timer — for anyone who can already solve: a speedcubing timer for the real cube. It shows the turns to mix the cube with (tap each one off as it is done), times the solve to a hundredth of a second, and keeps your best time and best average for each size, with a chart of how you are getting on. Touch and hold to get ready, let go to start, tap to stop — on the screen, or with the space bar. Stuck on a mix? Help Me Solve This Mix walks through it step by step.
    • My Real Cube — you paint in the colours of the cube in your hand and the app works out steps for the cube as it was painted in. If a colour is painted in wrong, the steps will be wrong too, so check each side before starting.

    Who it suits

    Anyone who wants to learn. There is no upper age limit: the lessons are the same for everyone, and grown-ups use them just as they are. The words are kept short and plain, so a confident reader of about seven can follow them alone; a younger child can do it all with an adult reading along, or with the Read button, which speaks every step aloud. Nothing in the app needs writing or spelling.

    How long it takes

    A lesson is five to ten minutes. A first full solve, with the app helping, often takes twenty to thirty minutes — the 3×3 walkthrough is about twenty-three cards. Everyone learns at their own pace; with regular practice, many go on to solve a 3×3 from memory. These are rough guides, not promises.

    Tips

    • A real cube in your hands makes it click. Any standard 3×3 will do; the app assumes white is opposite yellow, which is true of nearly all of them.
    • Tap Watch to see a step, then do the same on the real cube, then tap I Did It. Going one step at a time beats watching the whole solve.
    • Halfway through some tricks the bottom of the cube looks wrecked. That is normal and the app says so. Finish the trick and it comes back.
    • The middle squares never move. If you are stuck, it is almost always because the cube is being held a different way round — check the centres first.

    Safety

    • The app does not come with a cube. Real puzzle cubes have small parts that can be a choking hazard and are not for children under three; follow the age warning on the one you buy.
    • If a younger child is using the app, please stay nearby and decide together how long a session lasts. For everyone, short sessions with breaks work best.
    • The cube on screen turns and spins. If movement bothers you, turn on Reduce Motion in Settings › Accessibility › Motion, and stop if you feel unwell or dizzy.

    Getting on with it

    • Read speaks the current step; tapping it again stops it.
    • The guided solve plays at 🐢 Slow, 🚶 Steady or 🐇 Fast, and can be paused or stopped at any point.
    • With Reduce Motion turned on, the cube changes without the spinning animation.
    • Every colour is also named in words in every step, which helps anyone who finds red and green hard to tell apart.
    • Buttons are sized for a finger, and the app works with a keyboard as well: U, D, L, R, F and B turn a side, with Shift for the other direction.

    On iPad and iPhone

    • If a young child is using it, keep them in the app with Guided Access: turn it on in Settings › Accessibility › Guided Access, open Cube Clubhouse, then triple-click the side button (or the Home button) to start it.
    • Set a time limit with Screen Time, in Settings › Screen Time.
    • Bigger words: the app follows Settings › Display & Brightness › Text Size, and the larger Accessibility sizes too.
    • A clearer reading voice: the Read buttons use the voice chosen in Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content › Voices, where you can download an enhanced one.
    • Dark Mode: the app changes with the device, in Settings › Display & Brightness.

    Privacy

    This version of Cube Clubhouse (1.0) collects nothing about the people who use it and sends nothing about them anywhere. There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics, no purchases and no tracking of any kind.

    What is saved, and where

    FourFive small things are kept in this browser's own storage, on this device, inside the app, so the app is where you left it:

    • which lessons have been practised, their stars, and the 🧠 badge for any finished without hints;
    • the cube size last chosen on the Play screen;
    • the speed chosen for the guided solve;
    • the times from the Timer screen, so it can show your best: each time with its cube size and any +2 or DNF, plus the cube size last chosen there and whether 15 Seconds to Look First is switched on. No dates, no names.
    • for the update check: whether it is switched on, the date it last asked the App Store, and a version you tapped Not Now for, so it does not ask again that week.

    That is all of it. No names, no ages, no messages, no photos, no location, no identifiers. The app sends none of it anywhere and shares none of it between devices. The one way a copy leaves this device is inside your own backup of it — iCloud Backup, or a backup on your computer — if you make one. That backup belongs to your account; we never see it, and restoring it to a new device brings the stars along. Deleting the app, or tapping Clear Saved Progress above, erases what is on this device; an older backup keeps whatever it held when it was made, until you delete that backup.

    What the app asks the internet for

    Nothing. Not one request, not even for the typeface it is drawn in — that is stored inside the app like the lessons are.One question, to Apple, and you can switch it off. About once a day the app asks Apple's App Store whether a newer version of Cube Clubhouse is out, so it can offer the update. The question carries only the app's App Store name (its bundle identifier) and this device’s country or region, so it asks the right store. It carries nothing about the person using the app: no name, no progress, no times, and no identifier of anyone or of the device. Like any request on the internet it reaches Apple from your network's address; it never reaches us. Switch it off under About › Updates. Nothing else is ever requested, not even the typeface, which is stored inside the app like the lessons are. You can turn on Aeroplane Mode before anyone opens it and every part of the app still works; only the update check waits for a connection.

    We did not simply leave the requests out; the app is built to block them. The page carries a rule that forbids it from loading anything from the web, and on iPhone and iPad the app tells the system to refuse any web address on its behalf as well. The update check is made by the app itself, not by the page, and goes only to Apple. If a later version ever changes what the app does with information, we will update this page first.

    Nothing to buy

    Cube Clubhouse is free to use and has no purchases, no subscriptions and no adverts, so there is no payment for anyone to handle — no card details, no billing address, no account, nothing at all of that kind passing through the app.

    Reading aloud

    The Read buttons use the voice built into your device or browser, and only ever read the app's own instructions. On iPad and iPhone that voice runs on the device itself. Some desktop browsers offer voices that are produced online; if that matters to you, simply leave the button alone.

    What the app never touches

    No camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos or notifications. No cookies. No third-party code and no third-party servers: the one outside ingredient is the typeface, which is an open-source file stored inside the app and talks to nobody. The only server the app ever talks to is Apple's App Store, for the update check. The app does not show anything you do in it to anyone else.
