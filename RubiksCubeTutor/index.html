For parents

What this is

Cube Clubhouse teaches a child to solve a Cube on their own, using the beginner "layer by layer" method that most people learn first. It is a teaching app, not a game: there is no score to chase, no timer pressure, nothing to buy and nobody to talk to.

What is in it

  • Learn — ten lessons, in order, from "what the pieces are" to the last twist. Each one has a cube the child can turn, the trick written in words they can say out loud, and a practice cube that checks their work and awards up to three stars.
  • Play — a free-play cube from 2×2 up to 6×6, with mix-up, undo and a timer. Help me solve it walks any mixed cube back to solved, one card at a time, at a speed you pick, with pause and stop.
  • My real cube — the child paints in the colours of the cube in their hand and the app works out the steps for that exact cube.

Who it suits

The words are pitched at a confident reader of about seven and up. A younger child can do it all with an adult reading along, or with the Read button, which speaks every step aloud. Nothing in the app needs writing or spelling.

How long it takes

A lesson is five to ten minutes. A first full solve, with the app helping, usually takes twenty to thirty minutes — the 3×3 walkthrough is about twenty-three cards. After a week or two of practice most children can solve a 3×3 from memory in a few minutes.

Doing it together

  • A real cube in their hands makes it click. Any standard 3×3 will do; the app assumes white is opposite yellow, which is true of nearly all of them.
  • Press Watch to see a step, then do the same on the real cube, then press I did it. Going one step at a time beats watching the whole solve.
  • Halfway through some tricks the bottom of the cube looks wrecked. That is normal and the app says so. Finish the trick and it comes back.
  • The middle squares never move. If a child is stuck, it is almost always because the cube is being held a different way round — check the centres first.

What costs money

One thing, once: Help me solve it on a 3×3 or bigger — the walkthrough that shows every move of a solve. It is $0.99, it covers every cube size, and it carries across the devices signed in to the same account.

Free, and staying free: all ten lessons and their practice, the stars, mixing, undo, the timer, My real cube's colour check, and the whole 2×2 walkthrough — which teaches the same idea on a cube with no middles to worry about.

Getting on with it

  • Read speaks the current step; pressing it again stops it.
  • The guided solve plays at 🐢 Slow, 🚶 Steady or 🐇 Fast, and can be paused or stopped at any point.
  • If your device is set to reduce motion, the cube changes without the spinning animation.
  • Every colour is also named in words in every step, which helps a child who finds red and green hard to tell apart.
  • Buttons are sized for a finger, and the app works with a keyboard as well: U, D, L, R, F and B turn a side, with Shift for the other direction.

Privacy

Cube Clubhouse collects nothing about your child and sends nothing anywhere. There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking of any kind.

What is saved, and where

A few small things are kept in this browser's own storage, on this device, so the app is where the child left it:

  • which lessons have been practised, and their stars;
  • the cube size last chosen on the Play screen;
  • the speed chosen for the guided solve;
  • whether the step-by-step guide has been unlocked — a yes or a no, nothing more.

That is all of it. No names, no ages, no messages, no photos, no location, no identifiers. Nothing is sent off the device, and nothing is shared between devices. Removing the app, or pressing Clear saved progress above, erases it.

What the app asks the internet for

One thing: the page asks Google Fonts for the typeface it is drawn in. Like any web request, that tells Google the request happened and the address it came from. It carries nothing about your child and does not happen again once the font is cached. With no connection, or if that request is blocked, the app falls back to the font already on your device and works exactly the same. Nothing else — no lessons, no progress, no solving — needs a connection at all.

Paying for the step-by-step guide

The one purchase in the app goes through Apple's App Store. The app never sees a card number, a billing address or an Apple ID: it asks Apple whether this account has bought the unlock, and keeps the yes or no on the device. Nobody here can tell who bought it, and no purchase information is sent anywhere else.

Reading aloud

The Read button uses the voice built into your device or browser, and only ever reads the app's own instructions. On iPhone and iPad that voice runs on the device. Some desktop browsers offer voices that are produced online; if that matters to you, simply leave the button alone.

What the app never touches

No camera, microphone, location, contacts, photos or notifications. No cookies. No third-party code beyond the typeface above. Nothing your child does here can be seen by anyone else.

Children's privacy

Because nothing is collected, there is nothing to consent to, nothing to request and nothing for us to delete — it is all on your device, under your control.
